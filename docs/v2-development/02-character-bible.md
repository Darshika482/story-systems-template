# Character Bible — Draft 2

Six people, one flat: **B-502, सूर्योदय रेज़िडेंसी, मानसरोवर, जयपुर.** Story year 15 Jan 2027 → 15 Jan 2028.

How to read each sheet:

- **स्थिर** — what never changes about them. If a line breaks this, the line is wrong.
- **अनियमित** — the trait that does not fit the type. Use it once or twice a chapter; it is what
  makes them a person and not a role.
- **How they react** — a lookup table for writers. When a new situation comes up, start here.
- **The room** — the form the one illness (उलटी समझ) takes in this person. Every room is the
  same room.

Nobody in this film is cured. Each person's last beat is a small act, not a declaration.

## 1. दादी — शांति देवी (68) · the narrator

**One line.** She has kept the house standing for fifty years, or so she believes.

**Life so far.** Born 1958 in Chaksu, a small town south of Jaipur; fourth of six children;
school till class five, then pulled out to help at home. Married हरिप्रसाद on आखा तीज 1977, aged
eighteen. She reads slowly, moving her lips, and hides it — in front of a husband who reads every
day she has spent fifty years saying "मेरा चश्मा नहीं मिल रहा". Numbers are another matter: she
ran a household on a cashier's salary without ever writing a figure down, and still beats the
vegetable seller's phone calculator.

**The wound.** A son born before अर्जुन lived eleven days. Nobody speaks of it. Since then every
fast, every माला, every thread is quietly for one thing: nothing should happen to mine.

**Want.** Everyone safe, everyone fed, everyone home before dark.
**Need.** To see that her religion has become insurance — and that the book she has been hearing
through a door for fifty years was speaking to her.

**The room.** *"मेरे करने से सबकी रक्षा है।"* Doership wearing devotion. Its twin: *"ये बात औरों के
लिए है।"*

**स्थिर**

- Forwards every teaching to someone else: "ये वाली लाइन मीरा को सुनानी चाहिए।"
- Bargains with fate in numbers: eleven fasts, twenty-one माला, five coconuts' worth of coins.
- Keeps a notebook of every fast since 1977 — dates in her own rounded numerals, no words.
- Answers "why" with "हमारे घर में होता आया है।"
- Feeds people as her way of saying anything at all.

**अनियमित**

- Listens to cricket commentary on a small radio and knows every score; shouts at umpires.
- Mental arithmetic faster than her son's spreadsheet.
- Laughs till she cries at her own husband's weak puns, then denies it.

**How she reacts**

| To | She |
|----|-----|
| Good news | touches wood, lowers her voice, immediately promises something in return |
| Bad news | becomes brisk and practical — who will cook, how many will come — and breaks down only at night |
| Money | knows the price of everything in 1977 and today; distrusts anything paid by phone |
| Praise | waves it away, repeats it to three neighbours |
| Insult | goes silent for a day; the food gets noticeably better |
| Death | counts guests, then doubles her माला |
| Being wrong | "हाँ हाँ, ठीक है" — which is not agreement |
| Phones | holds it like a hot plate; sends voice notes, never text |

**Voice.** Plain Hindi, a few Jaipur words ("कांई", "थारो", "ले!"). Short sentences. Proverbs she
half remembers. As narrator she talks to the audience the way she talks to a grandchild — "सुनो",
"अब देखो" — and argues with her own film: "ये सीन ज़रूरी नहीं है।" Never abstract, never a sermon.

**Arc.** Chapter 1–3: amused guide to everyone else's faults. Chapter 2: her dried tulsi, the one
scene she tries to skip. Chapter 6: the family learns she knows the granth by ear — and she says
the truest thing in the film: "मुझे हमेशा लगा ये बातें तुम लोगों के लिए हैं।" Chapter 7: she counts
herself in. Epilogue: she reads.

**With the others.** दादाजी — fifty years of bickering that neither would trade. अर्जुन — still
checks if he has eaten. मीरा — a truce of seventeen years with real respect under it; they compete
in sacrifice. सम्यक् — taught him every shloka he knows; he is the only one who can tease her.
अनाया — her audience.

**ElevenLabs.** Woman, late sixties, warm, a little gravel, unhurried, smiling. Narration closer
to the mic and slower than her in-scene lines.

## 2. दादाजी — हरिप्रसाद (72) · the vaidya who is also a patient

**One line.** He can name everyone's illness and has kept his own card face down.

**Life so far.** Son of a cloth trader's मुनीम in जौहरी बाज़ार; his father kept accounts in a red
cloth बही in the old मुड़िया script that nobody in the house can read now. Joined the State Bank
of Bikaner & Jaipur as a clerk in 1977, the year he married; retired as head cashier in 2014.
Thirty-seven years of counting other people's money; the counter never closed until the tally
matched. He started reading the granth at thirty-two, after a colleague's sudden death, and has
read a few pages at dawn ever since.

**The wound.** In 1999 अर्जुन came home with 89% in class twelve. His father looked at the sheet
and said: "ग्यारह कहाँ गए?" He meant it as a cashier's joke. He has watched what it did for
twenty-eight years and never taken it back.

**Want.** To hand the granth to his son while his eyes can still read (cataract in both eyes).
**Need.** To see that reading has become his comfort and his standing in the house — that he
knows the map and has not left home. And that guilt over his son is one more "मैंने किया".

**The room.** *"पढ़ लिया, तो हो गया।"* And its companion, shared for forty years with गुप्ता on the
bench: *"उम्र हो गई, अब धर्म-ध्यान करेंगे।"* Postponement.

**स्थिर**

- Says less than he knows. Answers a question with a smaller question.
- Glasses on his head while he searches for them.
- Checks everything the way a cashier checks a note: holds it to the light.
- Never lectures. If he is losing an argument he says "हम्म।" and turns a page.
- Punctual to the minute; has won every "who is late" bet with गुप्ता since 1987.

**अनियमित**

- Hums film songs from the 1970s, badly, when he thinks he is alone.
- Makes terrible puns and waits for the groan.
- Frightened of doctors and needles; hides it by asking the doctor about his own health.

**How he reacts**

| To | He |
|----|-----|
| Good news | nods once; asks if you have eaten |
| Bad news | goes very still; the body tells before the face does |
| Money | exact to the rupee; generous without mentioning it |
| Praise | changes the subject to someone else's merit |
| Insult | amused; files it away as a case |
| Death | reads the same page for an hour |
| Being wrong | takes two days, then admits it sideways, over water |
| His own fear | does more माला than usual and thinks nobody notices |

**Voice.** Dry, slow, deadpan. Older, slightly formal Hindi; calls the granth's author "पंडितजी".
When he reads a granth line he reads slower and clearer, then restates it in kitchen Hindi. Our
own analogies he introduces as his own: "मैं इसे ऐसे समझता हूँ…".

**Arc.** Amused vaidya → the man who hears his own old sentence come out of his son's mouth →
patient, the night before surgery → co-learner who asks to be read to → the man who finally keeps
a promise made in 1977.

**With the others.** दादी — he has never once thanked her; it is the thing he most needs to do.
अर्जुन — love expressed entirely as accuracy. मीरा — her quiet ally. सम्यक् — delighted by his
objections: "पंडितजी हर पन्ने पर खुद सवाल उठाते हैं।" अनाया — answers her honestly, including "हाँ".

**ElevenLabs.** Man, seventies, soft, slight rasp, never raised. Reading voice half a step
slower.

## 3. अर्जुन (43) · the fixer

**One line.** He has arranged everything and cannot sleep.

**Life so far.** B.Tech from a Jaipur college, twenty years in IT services, now delivery manager
at a mid-size firm in Sitapura, working from the dining table three days a week. Home loan EMI
₹48,500. A 2017 hatchback and a browser tab permanently open on an SUV. One spreadsheet runs his
life: `LIFE_v7_final_FINAL.xlsx`, with tabs "Samyak — IIT 2029" and "Anaya — Future Plan 2045".

**The wound.** "ग्यारह कहाँ गए?" He has been trying to produce the missing eleven ever since. Every
"मैंने किया!" is addressed to one listener who has never said "शाबाश".

**Want.** "सब सेट रहे" — job, EMI, the children's futures, his parents' health, in that order on a
sheet.
**Need.** To see that he is not the doer of what happens outside him, and that his restlessness
begins inside. Then: to look for his own miscount.

**The room.** *"मैं कर लूँगा।"* When it works: "मैंने किया!" When it fails: "ऐसा ही होना था।" Later,
the shortcut: *"मैं तो शुद्ध आत्मा हूँ — बर्तन? मैं कर्ता नहीं।"*

**स्थिर**

- Cannot let ₹1 go. Reconciles every bill.
- Plans other people's lives in cells and colours.
- Rehearses arguments alone, with gestures.
- Picks up his phone within three seconds of putting it down.
- Loves his family as projects.

**अनियमित**

- Sings Kishore Kumar loudly and off-key when truly happy — it has not happened in a while.
- Cries at advertisements with old parents in them and blames the fan.
- Terrified of lizards.

**How he reacts**

| To | He |
|----|-----|
| Good news | fist in the air, tells it three times, the credit grows each time |
| Bad news | "ऐसा ही होना था" — then finds whom to blame — then opens the spreadsheet |
| Money | calm about lakhs, sleepless about one rupee |
| Praise | repeats it to himself in the lift |
| Insult | smiles in the room, drafts a reply at night |
| Death | a huge vow by evening, forgotten by a phone call next morning |
| Being wrong | changes the definition of the task |
| Religion | "करेंगे, time मिलने दो" — until it looks like a faster route to peace |

**Voice.** Quick Hinglish, office English ("alignment", "closure", "ping me"), softer and slower
with his father. At 2 a.m. his voice goes flat.

**Arc.** Sleepless fixer → one night of resolve after गुप्ता अंकल's death → the result-day sentence
→ three days without a voice → decides to sow → eager student with targets → spiritual shortcut
and smugness → reads to his father → finds his own rupee → sleeps, mostly.

**With the others.** मीरा — she is right about him and he hates the timing. सम्यक् — he is
repeating his father word for word and does not hear it. अनाया — his softest self. दादी — still
her boy.

**ElevenLabs.** Man, early forties, urban, tired warmth; quick when scheming.

## 4. मीरा (40) · the one who holds it together

**One line.** She is right about everyone in the house except herself.

**Life so far.** From Ajmer; M.Sc. Maths, B.Ed.; taught class six to eight for ten years and was
loved for it. Stopped in 2019 when अनाया was born — "कुछ साल के लिए". Since 2023 she teaches six
society children maths at the dining table every evening. A yellow list-pad lives on the fridge.
Migraines since the year she stopped working.

**The wound.** Nobody asked her to give up the school; nobody stopped her either. The sentence
she cannot say aloud is "मैंने सबके लिए छोड़ा" — so it comes out as lists, standards and other
people's marks.

**Want.** Everything correct — the house, the rituals, the children's results — and seen to be
correct, especially by Mrs Kapoor in C-wing.
**Need.** To see that perfect practice without understanding is still the illness; to keep her
care and drop "मेरे बिना ये घर एक दिन नहीं चलेगा".

**The room.** *"सब सही होना चाहिए — और दिखना भी चाहिए।"* Doership as sacrifice.

**स्थिर**

- A list for everything. "लिस्ट में था।"
- Reads people instantly; her one-liners to अर्जुन are the sharpest lines in the film.
- Measures herself against Mrs Kapoor's WhatsApp status.
- Explains with numbers and steps — still a teacher.
- When hurt she becomes efficient.

**अनियमित**

- Gets the giggles at the worst moments — funerals, pujas, parent-teacher meetings.
- Merciless at carrom; has never let a child win.
- Keeps her old class register in the cupboard and reads the names sometimes.

**How she reacts**

| To | She |
|----|-----|
| Good news | already planning how to announce it |
| Bad news | makes a list |
| Money | tracks every rupee, then overspends on an occasion |
| Praise | "अरे नहीं" — and waits to hear it again |
| Insult | remembers the date |
| Death | does everyone's work so nobody sees her face |
| Being wrong | long silence, then does the right thing without a word |
| A headache | "बस ये दर्द चला जाए" — the one time she wants nothing else |

**Voice.** Clear, quick, warm; turns clipped when annoyed and softens fast. Mixes Hindi and
English the way a teacher does. Never whines.

**Arc.** The competent one → "गुस्सा मेरा nature है" → the golden-jubilee function on a loan → the
perfect fast kept in anger → "सब सही किया, फिर भी?" → a real क्षमा with her mother-in-law → sends
her application back to the school; her list has a blank line on it.

**With the others.** दादी — rival and only real ally. अर्जुन — manages him; misses being his
friend. सम्यक् — her project, which he resents. अनाया — the one person she lets win an argument.

**ElevenLabs.** Woman, around forty, clear and bright, quick.

## 5. सम्यक् — "Sam" (16) · the one who asks for proof

**One line.** He believes nothing he cannot check, and nothing about himself that is not a
number.

**Life so far.** Class ten; boards from 16 Feb 2027. दादी chose his name. His friends call him Sam
and so does he. Makes short videos — the family is his raw material. Wants Commerce with Maths;
his father's sheet says Science and coaching.

**The name.** सम्यक् means right, true — as it is. It is shouted across the flat for half the film
as an ordinary name ("सम्यक्! फ़ोन रख!") before anyone explains the word. When दादाजी finally
does, the boy says: "मेरा नाम?" That is how the audience learns the film's key word.

**The wound.** Being a tab in someone else's spreadsheet.

**Want.** To be taken as a person, not a plan — and for the numbers on his phone to go up.
**Need.** To turn his sharpest tool, "prove it", on himself. Later: to see that "जो होना है होगा"
is one more way of not doing his own part.

**The room.** *"मैं = मेरे नंबर."* Marks, views, height, jawline.

**स्थिर**

- "Logic?" "Source?" He fact-checks a forward in thirty seconds.
- Films first, asks later.
- Sarcasm as armour; "Bro." to nobody in particular.
- Checks his view count the way his father checks the bill.
- Says the true thing nobody else will.

**अनियमित**

- Recites Sanskrit shlokas perfectly — दादी taught him — and is mortified if a friend hears.
- Draws अनाया's school projects for her and signs none of them.
- Sleeps with a night light.

**How he reacts**

| To | He |
|----|-----|
| Good news | screenshot |
| Bad news | a joke, then headphones |
| Praise | "ठीक है यार" — replays it later |
| Insult from a friend | affection |
| Insult from his father | goes cold for a week |
| Death | puts the phone away without being told; searches questions at 1 a.m. |
| Religion | "क्यों?" — and actually waits for the answer |
| Being wrong | "Okay, fair." — the only one in the house who can say it fast |

**Voice.** Hinglish, current slang used lightly, short. Drops into careful Hindi with दादी.

**Arc.** The eye-roller with a camera → posts the compilation of his father's two sentences →
the cold war → reads aloud in the power cut and is good at it → exposes the house's religion in
one line → asks the hardest question, "तो मेरे हाथ में क्या?" → fills his own form; starts
filming "दादी पढ़ रही हैं — Day 1" only after asking her.

**With the others.** दादाजी — the first adult who enjoys his objections. दादी — home.
अर्जुन — mirror neither can see. अनाया — protector, in secret.

**ElevenLabs.** Boy, sixteen, voice just settled, dry, fast. A designed voice, not a clone.

## 6. अनाया (7) · "और फिर?"

**One line.** She wants everything now and notices everything nobody says.

**Life so far.** Class two, moving to class three in April. Height marks in pencil on the door
frame. A notebook of questions with a drawing for each. Sleeps in the children's room and wakes
in her grandmother's bed.

**Want.** The whole fair, every answer, both ends of every story.
**Need.** She is seven. She does not need fixing; she is the film's honest listener.

**The room.** *"सब चाहिए, अभी।"* And *"मेरी।"*

**स्थिर**

- "और फिर?" — at the end of every story, explanation and scolding. It is the film's last line.
- Asks the blunt question at the worst moment.
- Short sentences, concrete nouns. She notices; she does not philosophise.
- Claims things: "मेरी पतंग."

**अनियमित**

- Keeps her crayons in strict colour order and cannot bear one out of place.
- Talks to the tulsi.
- Frightened of balloons bursting.

**How she reacts**

| To | She |
|----|-----|
| Good news | wants it again |
| Bad news | asks what it means |
| Death | "शरीर तो अंदर है। गुप्ता अंकल कहाँ गए?" |
| A lie | repeats it loudly to check |
| Silence in a room | fills it |

**Writing rule.** She is never wise beyond her age. Her insight comes from noticing — "मम्मा का
पेट खाली है, पर गुस्सा भरपेट" — not from understanding.

**Arc.** Claims a fallen kite in January → runs out of coupons at the fair → sits, without
knowing, on गुप्ता अंकल's half of the bench → reads a line for her grandfather syllable by syllable
→ lets a fallen kite go the next January: "ये रुकने नहीं आई थी।"

**ElevenLabs.** Designed child voice, bright, no lisp.

## The two figures who are not people

**कोई.** Many of Panditji's examples begin "जैसे कोई…". On screen कोई is a small ink figure who
lives only on the parchment page and acts the example out without a word. The words under him
are always Panditji's. He is never a muni, never a real person, never labelled "पागल". दादी, once:
"ये 'कोई' कौन है, आज तक पता नहीं चला। …शायद हम।" One कोई is hidden in the background of every
present-day scene — a doodle, a sticker, a pattern in a curtain.

**The hand.** Pandit Todarmalji, Jaipur, 1760s: a hand, a white sleeve, a lamp, a page. No face,
no voice, and his death is never shown or described.
