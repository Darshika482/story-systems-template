## 2. The three questions, answered

### How many people in the family?

Six, in three generations. This is the household most of the audience grew up in or still
lives in: grandparents who stayed, a couple in the middle carrying the EMI, a teenager on a
phone, a small child who asks everything aloud. Each viewer finds their own age in the frame.
A family of four reads as "urban couple with a problem"; a family of six reads as "हमारा घर".

Six also lets the film do what the granth does: show **one illness wearing six different
faces**. Nobody is the villain and nobody is the guru.

### How religious should they be?

As religious as most Indian homes actually are — by habit. Enough practice that the second half
("धर्म के भेष में भूल") has something real to examine, never so devout that an ordinary viewer
feels this is somebody else's house.

| Person | Their religion in one line |
|--------|---------------------------|
| दादी | does everything, has never opened the book: "पढ़ना इनका काम है, मैं तो बस करती हूँ" |
| दादाजी | reads daily for forty years; knows the map, has not walked it |
| मीरा | keeps every rule correctly, and needs it to be seen |
| अर्जुन | festival-religious; "time नहीं है"; later takes a shortcut and calls it spirituality |
| सम्यक् | "Logic क्या है?" — and turns out to be the listener the granth actually wants |
| अनाया | folds her hands when told, then asks why |

The family is Jain by birth and it shows lightly — दस लक्षण, क्षमावाणी, no eating after dark,
"जय जिनेन्द्र" on the phone. The film never explains these as doctrine; they are "हमारे घर में
होता आया है", which is exactly how a family of any faith holds its customs. A viewer who keeps
Navratri or Roza will map it onto their own house without being asked to.

### Does the film end in स्व की खोज?

Yes — but it ends with the search **starting**, not with anyone arriving. Nobody is declared
enlightened; that would be false to the granth and false to life. What changes over the year is
the **direction of looking**.

That is why the story runs from one मकर संक्रांति to the next. On संक्रांति the sun does not
change; it changes direction. In the first scene the whole city is looking up and out — at kites,
at other people's roofs, at phones. In the last scene the house is quiet and one person is
looking down at a page, at her own life.

दादी's closing line carries it:

> "साल भर पहले हमारे घर को रात में नींद नहीं आती थी। अब नींद तो आ जाती है… हम दिन में जागना सीख
> रहे हैं। पहुँचे कहीं नहीं हैं। बस दिशा बदली है।"

## 3. Why दादी narrates

- She is the family's memory, and an Indian audience already knows how to listen to a
  grandmother telling a story. The film opens as exactly that: a bedtime story to अनाया.
- She is funny about everyone, including herself: "मैं शांति। नाम पर मत जाना।"
- **She has a blind spot, and it is the film's last reveal.** For fifty years she has heard the
  granth through the kitchen door while दादाजी read. She knows half of it by ear. And every line
  she ever heard, she passed on in her head to someone else — "ये बात बहू को सुननी चाहिए". All
  through the film she counts everyone's illness. Near the end she finds the count is one short:
  the one who was counting.
- So the narrator is not a voice above the story. She is the last patient, and in the final scene
  she is the one at the kitchen table at 2 a.m., reading the first line aloud, slowly, with her
  finger under the words.

अनाया's direct address to camera from Draft 1 is dropped; two devices that break the frame
compete with each other. अनाया keeps her bluntness inside the scenes.

## 4. The new ending, in plain steps

1. **Climax — 2 a.m. again.** For a year अर्जुन has complained that the society bill is ₹2,841
   and he pays ₹2,840: "एक रुपया कहाँ गया?" He finally checks his own standing instruction. The
   rupee was never taken. He had set the amount himself, three years ago. He laughs — the first
   real laugh of the film. This is the granth's own image (p. 259): one whose account does not
   tally "अपनी चूकको ढूँढता है". And it is his father's rule from thirty-seven years as a bank
   cashier: when the tally fails, recount your own drawer first.
2. **Diwali — the unfinished page.** दादाजी, with his new lens, turns to the last page of the
   granth. It stops on half a word: "बहु". The word was "बहुरि" — "और फिर…". It is the phrase
   अनाया has been saying all film: "और फिर?"
3. **The fifty-year promise.** On his wedding night in 1977 दादाजी promised to teach दादी to read
   properly. He hands her his old thick glasses: "एक वादा था। सिर पर ही रखा रह गया।" The
   glasses-on-the-head gag from minute four turns into the heart of the film.
4. **Epilogue — संक्रांति 2028, then 2 a.m.** The house is dark. No phone glow. One light in the
   kitchen: दादी, the old glasses sliding down her nose, sounding out "मं-ग-ल-म-य". अर्जुन comes
   for water, sits, and slides her the steel glass — exactly what his father did for him a year
   earlier.
5. **Last sound.** The Jaipur lamp, the page with "बहु", and a sleepy child: "और फिर, दादी?" —
   "और फिर… वो तू बताएगी।" Black.

The audience gets a real emotional release (a husband keeping a fifty-year-old promise, a son
who can sleep) while the philosophical ending stays honest and open.

## 5. What the references taught us

| Reference | What we take | Where it shows |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| Yeh Meri Family | a warm narrator inside the family; small domestic events treated as large; exact period detail | दादी's voice; ₹2,841 bills, steel glasses with names engraved, the society WhatsApp group |
| Gullak | "किस्से, कहानी नहीं"; bickering as the language of love; nobody says "I love you", they say "खाना खाया?" | every reconciliation in this film is a glass of water or "खाना खा ले" |
| The Haunting of Hill House | one house, each chapter belongs to one family member; the reveal that every room was the same room; hidden figures in the background | each chapter has a focal character; the Nidan Board reveal that every case has one root; a hidden कोई in every scene |
| 3 Idiots | narrator who thinks he is telling someone else's story; the message arrives as a joke first | दादी; "चश्मा सिर पर है" |
| Piku, Kapoor & Sons | ageing parents written without sentiment; one family fight where everyone is partly right | the anniversary function; the उत्तम क्षमा day fight |
| Inside Out, Bluey | an abstract idea becomes a small wordless figure; the feeling lands in a nearly silent moment | कोई on the parchment page; ten quiet seconds before every closing line |

Indian-audience rule drawn from all of them: **the specific is the universal.** We do not make
the family vague to include everyone; we make it exact so that everyone recognises it.

## 6. Tone and dialogue

- **Light by default.** Most scenes are banter over a household task. People interrupt, mishear,
  change the subject.
- **Heavy is rare and quiet.** One still scene per chapter. No music under it. The shot after
  bad news holds six seconds.
- **The core line is thrown away, never announced.** It is said while shelling peas or looking
  for glasses, in ten words or fewer, and nobody in the scene remarks on it.
- **Nobody states a feeling in a tidy sentence.** Nobody summarises. No two characters agree in
  paragraphs.
- **दादाजी never wins by authority.** He shows, asks, or stays silent. He never opens with
  "बेटा, जीवन में…".
- **We laugh at the "मैं", never at** dharma, munis, other paths, bodies, or the granth's words.
  The joke comes first; then quiet; then Panditji's line.

## 7. Built for code animation and ElevenLabs

Both limits are treated as style, not as handicap.

- **The dollhouse.** The flat is drawn once as a cut-away cross-section. In wide frame we see
  three rooms side by side; **each room is exactly one 9:16 column**, so the same render gives
  the film frame and the reel frame. At 2 a.m. the audience sees the whole house at once: two
  blue rooms (father and son on their phones), one gold room (दादाजी's lamp). The "one illness in
  every room" idea becomes a picture.
- **Few rigs, used fully.** Six family rigs. Everyone else is a voice, a phone screen, a
  silhouette on the next balcony, or hands at a door.
- **Three cheap, beautiful worlds:** the flat (limited animation, strong faces), the parchment
  page where कोई acts out Panditji's "जैसे कोई…" examples in ink, and Jaipur in the 1760s — a
  hand, a lamp, a page, never a face.
- **Phones are free animation.** Text bubbles, status rings, a spreadsheet tab — they move the
  story and cut straight into reels.
- **Voices.** Six designed voices plus a small pool for off-screen people. Lines are short, with
  punctuation doing the timing. No scene depends on two people talking over each other.
- **Props are code.** Every prop has one spec (shape, colour, wear marks, states) and one drawing.
  दादाजी's glasses are the same glasses in scene 2 and scene 62. See `05-prop-bible.md`.

## 8. Reels

Each numbered scene is written to stand alone in 90 seconds or less: a hook in the first two
seconds, at most one granth card, and दादी's closing question to the viewer ("आपके घर में चश्मा
किसके सिर पर है?"). Reel markers sit in the screenplay as notes. सम्यक् also films on his phone
inside the story, which gives the film native vertical footage and a reason for it.

## 9. Still open — your call

- **Title.** The working title stays मोक्षमार्गप्रकाशक. For a mass release consider a friendlier
  main title with the granth named beneath it, e.g. *चश्मा सिर पर है* or *और फिर…*.
- **Surname.** The script uses "जैन" only on the nameplate. It can be removed entirely.
- **Kites.** The family does not fly kites (the glass-coated string hurts birds); a cut kite
  lands on their terrace instead. If you want them flying with plain cotton thread, one scene
  changes.
